Q3. What happens to the bounding boxes with update + fullRefit?

The bounding boxes stay correct, since every box still encloses all of its circles, but they
get worse over time. fullRefit only resizes the boxes and never changes the tree structure, so
circles stay grouped with the neighbours they had when the tree was first built. As the circles
drift apart, the boxes for those groups stretch to cover circles that are now far from each
other. After a few seconds most internal boxes span large parts of the screen and overlap heavily,
until the hierarchy is almost meaningless. Rendering still gives the right image, but a pixel
now lands inside many overlapping boxes, so tracing visits many more nodes and circle tests and
the BVH stops speeding things up.

Q4. What happens with reset (rebuild every frame) instead?

The tree is rebuilt with the SAH binning every frame from the circles' current positions. The
boxes stay tight around nearby circles and overlap very little, just like the BVH on the first
frame, no matter how long the simulation runs. The box layout changes from frame to frame, and
it can jump suddenly when a circle crosses a split plane and gets regrouped with different
neighbours. The cost is building the whole tree, O(n log n), every frame instead of the O(n)
refit. With 100 circles that is cheap compared with tracing 4 samples x 1024 x 768 pixels, so
traversal stays fast while a refit-only BVH keeps getting slower.

Q5. Why are some circles drawn with their sides cut off?

There are two causes:
1. Circle centres are chosen uniformly in [0, width] x [0, height] and the radius is ignored.
   A circle whose centre is less than one radius from the window border hangs partly off screen,
   so the window clips that side flat. Moving circles (Q3) make this worse because they can drift
   off screen. Fix: spawn centres in [r, width - r] x [r, height - r] and make circles bounce off
   the walls in Scene::update.
2. Each pixel was sampled at its top-left corner (x, y) instead of its centre
   (x + 0.5, y + 0.5). That shifts the traced image half a pixel relative to the boxes drawn on
   top of it. A box's 1px outline sits just outside its max edge, so it is rasterised onto the
   last pixel column or row that is still inside the circle and paints over it. The circle then
   looks flat where it touches the box's right or bottom edge. Fix: sample around the pixel
   centre (the antialiasing samples become (x + 0.5 +- 0.3333, y + 0.5 +- 0.3333)). Circle pixels
   and outline pixels then no longer overlap.
Both fixes are in main.cpp.
